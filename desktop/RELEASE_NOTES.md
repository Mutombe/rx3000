The screen tells you what it is doing.

**Pressing a button shows that it worked**

Settling something awaiting payment closed the dialogue and then showed
nothing until the server answered, so the row sat looking exactly as it had a
moment earlier. That is a row somebody presses again, and taking the money
twice is the one mistake a till must not make easy.

The row now says "Taking the money" where the buttons were, and the buttons go
while it does. The rest of the till carries on: you can take the money on
three sales one after another without waiting for any of them.

The same is true of sending a delivery out, signing one off, marking one
undelivered, handing over or cancelling a lay by, approving an order, sending
one to a supplier, reversing a sale, replying on the help desk and retiring a
driver.

**A delivery shows how far it has got**

Raised, out with a driver, signed for at the door: three marks on the row,
filled behind and hollow ahead. A round of forty waybills can be read for the
ones still moving without reading a word. A delivery that failed is ringed
rather than filled, because it did not arrive.

**Figures sit together**

The numbers above a list were four separate boxes saying four unrelated
things. They are one panel now, divided by fine rules, which is what they
always were: what is due, what is overdue, what cannot be filled, how many
there are. It takes about a third less room.

**A changed script reads as the change**

What it used to say, struck through, then what it says now. It was two columns
to compare across a gap, on the one screen where what it used to say is the
whole reason for looking.

**Several cases at once**

Tick them on the help desk and close them together. A case answered at the
counter and never closed is closed on a quiet afternoon or not at all, and one
at a time means not at all.

**Also**

Every record page lays out its identifiers in the same places, so you learn
once where the telephone number is rather than hunting for it on each kind of
record. A reply that fails to send now hands your words back instead of losing
them.
