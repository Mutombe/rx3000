The tables.

**Every list now shows people as faces**

A patient, a prescriber, a driver, a cashier. A name is read letter by letter;
a face is recognised before it is read, which is the difference when you are
looking down a column of thirty for one of them. It matters here more than
most places: a repeats queue can hold four people called Moyo, and four
identical lines is a list nobody can navigate.

Nobody picks the face and nothing is stored. It is drawn from the name, so one
person is the same face on every screen. Somebody who is not recorded gets no
face and a sentence instead, because a blank face for an absent prescriber is
the software inventing a person.

**One row, one line**

A hundred and fifty-eight cells put a second line under the value: a telephone
number under a name, a payment state under an invoice, a schedule under a
medicine. A row was one line tall or two depending on whether that record
happened to have a telephone number, so rows no longer lined up and the eye
could not track across a wide table.

Every row is now one line and every row is the same height. There is a detail
page for every row in the system; what does not fit on the line is one click
away.

On the dispensing history the patient column had gone further and was stacking
two different people, the patient above and the prescriber below. That column
is the patient now.

**The row tells you what it is doing**

Settling something that was awaiting payment closed the dialogue and then
showed nothing until the server answered, so the row sat looking exactly as it
had a moment earlier. That is a row somebody presses again, and taking the
money twice is the one mistake a till must not make easy.

The row now says "Taking the money" where the buttons were, and the buttons go
while it does. The rest of the till carries on: you can take the money on
three sales one after another without waiting for any of them. The same is
true of sending a delivery out, signing one off, marking one undelivered, and
handing over or cancelling a lay by.

**Everything above a table is one instrument**

The search, the filters and the count used to be three different shapes
depending which screen you were on. They are one bordered rail now, and the
number of results sits at the end of it rather than somewhere else on the
page.

**Column names carry a mark**

A small icon before each one: a person, a date, a figure, a medicine. A header
row is the hardest text on a screen to read, and the mark is what lets you
find the money column without reading any of them. The same mark everywhere,
because it comes from the column's own name.

**A table with nothing in it says which nothing it is**

Some tables simply ended, leaving a header above a blank space, which reads as
a screen that failed to load rather than a shop with nothing to show. They now
say whether there has never been one of these, or whether the filters have
excluded them all.

**Also**

Every page heading is built the same way and carries the size of its list. A
price review that showed the first twenty five of a longer list now says so.
